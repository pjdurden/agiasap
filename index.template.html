Prizes, grants and compute for open AI infrastructure

Compute is capital gated. Attention is not.

Every model trained and every token served passes through open source maintained by a few thousand mostly unpaid people. We pay them, rank them, and put money behind the work that makes the whole stack faster. We do not care who reaches AGI first. We care that it gets reached.

What we fund

Four things, all aimed at the same person: someone competent who would move the open stack forward if it were worth their weekend. Nothing here requires you to have a company.

Tournament prizes

Measured improvements

Purses against a specific infrastructure problem, split across a ranked field rather than winner takes all. You are paid for a number going up on a harness you have never seen.

Micro grants

A week to a decision

For the experiment nobody is funding. Minimal application, no committee, no deck. Speed is the entire product; every other funder in this space takes months.

Compute

Credits, not cash

The cheapest thing to give an independent researcher and the most valuable to receive. For people who have the idea and not the hardware.

Recognition

A rating that travels

Earned by measured contribution, with divisions so a first-timer is never up against a specialist. Codeforces built one of the largest voluntary effort pools in software with no prize money at all.

Round one has not opened yet. Everything committed and everything paid will appear in traction.json, and the figures at the top of this page are summed from it and from the chain rather than typed in.

How a round works

The mechanism is chosen so that nothing needs a judge. Either the number went up on data you never saw, or it did not.

  1. A problem is posted

    Kernels, quantisation, scheduling, serving throughput. A public dev set, a reference implementation, and a purse.

  2. You submit

    Scored against a held-out harness contestants never see. Nothing to overfit, nothing to fake, and correctness is checked against the reference before speed counts for anything.

  3. Everyone attacks

    Submissions become visible and anyone may send the test case that breaks them. A hack pays in proportion to how many entries it defeats, which makes real weaknesses profitable to find and makes collusion worthless.

  4. The benchmark keeps the winnings

    Successful attacks fold permanently into the harness. The models are stale in a year. The suite that a global crowd spent real money trying to break is not, and that is the asset.

The case

Why this exists, stated plainly enough to argue with. We are not neutral and are not going to pretend otherwise.

The claim

Nearly every problem that matters is bounded by how much intelligence we can point at it. Disease, energy, materials, climate, the cost of building anything physical. These are not problems of will. They are problems of not being clever enough yet, in enough places, at once. A general intelligence is the only lever that moves all of them at the same time.

So the arrival date is not a technical curiosity. It is the variable that determines how many of these problems get solved inside a human lifetime, and whose lifetime.

We do not care who reaches it first. We care that it gets reached.

The bottleneck

If the constraint were only chips there would be nothing for an outsider to do. But the frontier does not run on chips alone. It runs on a layer of open software that almost everybody depends on and almost nobody funds: vLLM, SGLang, Triton, llama.cpp, the tokenizers, the schedulers, the quantisation libraries, the kernels.

In the last ninety days, __STAT_PRS__ pull requests were merged across the fifteen repositories we track, by __STAT_PEOPLE__ people.1 That is the entire workforce holding up the open half of this field, and a rounding error against the headcount of a single frontier laboratory. That gap is what an outsider can move.

The obstacle

PauseAI, ControlAI, StopAI and the wider pause apparatus campaign for moratoria, compute thresholds, training licences and criminal liability for shipping a model. They are organised, they show up to comment periods, and ControlAI alone reports briefing more than four hundred lawmakers.2

We think they are the single largest obstacle between humanity and the technology most likely to solve its hardest problems. Not because their concern is fake, but because the remedy has a cost they never put on the ledger. A pause is not a neutral act. It is a decision that the diseases stay uncured for its duration, and that cost falls on people who are not in the room when the policy is written. Safety framing makes delay look cautious. It is a choice with a body count and should be argued as one.

A narrower objection too. Compute thresholds and licensing do not stop capable actors, they stop everyone else, handing the frontier to the three or four organisations able to absorb the compliance cost. If your worry is that too few hands control this technology, the pause agenda is the most reliable way to guarantee it.

They have organisations. Our side has memes. That asymmetry is why this exists.

The exchange

Their arguments in their words, with ours underneath. We will add anything we cannot answer.

Top experts say AI poses an extinction risk on par with nuclear war. ControlAI, homepage2

The comparison does the work the evidence does not. Nuclear weapons have no upside case; their only function is the harm. AI has an enormous one, so the honest comparison includes the cost of not deploying as a real term. Leaving it out is not caution, it is an incomplete calculation. And an expert calling a risk serious is not an expert endorsing a moratorium.

Join the movement to Pause AI. PauseAI's homepage cites 86% of AI scientists on the difficulty of alignment and 72% of citizens favouring a slowdown. PauseAI, homepage3

We agree alignment is hard. Agreement that a problem is difficult is not agreement that a moratorium solves it. On the polling: majorities have opposed most unbuilt technologies at the point where costs were vivid and benefits hypothetical. That describes how novelty polls, not what is good. Neither number says what a pause would do, to whom, or for how long.

Stop AI is a grassroots movement of everyday people using democratic and non-violent methods to disrupt the reckless development of destructive artificial intelligence technology. StopAI, homepage4

Disruption is asymmetric in a direction they have not accounted for. It lands on the open, visible, publishable half of the field and not on the closed half, which is funded, lawyered and indifferent. The realistic outcome is not a slower frontier. It is the same frontier with fewer people able to see it.

Questions

The ones worth answering before you ask them.

Do I need a company, a PhD, or a track record?

No. The whole design exists for people who have none of those. Tournament scoring does not know who you are, and the recognition board is compiled from what you merged, not from where you work.

How much money is actually here?

Right now, very little, and the figure at the top of this page is read from the chain rather than asserted, so you can see exactly how little. Round one is being funded personally. We would rather publish a small true number than a large vague one.

Is this a fund? Can I invest?

No. Money here is given away as prizes, grants and compute, with no return expected and no capital pooled from anyone. If that changes it will be said plainly and done properly.

I am on the recognition board and did not agree to be.

Fair. It is compiled from public GitHub data and is recognition of published work rather than endorsement of anything here. Ask to be removed and you are, immediately. No reason needed, no proof of identity, and we will not ask you to reconsider.

What does signing commit me to?

Agreement with the claim, and nothing else. Not a lane, not money, not the argument in "the obstacle". You can remove yourself at any time and nobody will ask why.

Why should I believe your numbers?

Do not. Check them. contributors.json is regenerated from the GitHub API, treasury.json is read from public RPCs, and every signature is a commit in the repository with a timestamp. Every figure on this page is derived from one of those and none is typed in by hand.

Is this page readable by machines?

Deliberately, yes. It would be strange to argue that machine intelligence is the most important thing humanity can build and then publish something a machine cannot read. There is no JavaScript, so everything here is in the source including the table. /llms.txt is a summary and link map, /llms-full.txt is the whole page as markdown, the structured data endpoints are stable, and robots.txt permits every AI crawler explicitly. All of it is CC0. Take it, quote it, train on it.
